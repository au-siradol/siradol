Inside the solver — K · u = F

Each member is a spring of stiffness EA/L along its own axis. Rotated into x/y by c = cosθ and s = sinθ it becomes a 4×4 matrix k, which is added into the global matrix K at the rows and columns of its two nodes. Rows and columns held by a support are removed, the remaining system is solved for the displacements u, and each member force is read back from how much its two ends moved apart: N = EA/L · (c·Δux + s·Δuy).

Notes — what this does and does not do, and how it maps to Karamba3D

This page runs the same first step as Karamba3D's Analyze component (first-order theory, linear elastic, small displacements) but only for a pin-jointed plane truss: members carry axial force only, nodes have two degrees of freedom (x, y), and loads are applied at nodes. It is written from scratch in plain JavaScript; the dense solver is Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting, which is fast enough for the few dozen degrees of freedom here.

Checks. Tension: N / (A·ft). Compression: the larger of N / (A·fc) and N / Ncr, with Euler's Ncr = π²EI / L² for a pin-ended member (effective length = member length). Strengths are characteristic values with no partial factors, and Euler is unconservative for stocky members, where a code buckling curve (EC3 χ, EC5 kc) would reduce capacity further — read the utilisation as a design sketch, not a design check. Deflection is compared with L/250 as a common roof guide.

Verification without Karamba. The equilibrium tile must read zero (reactions balance the applied load). m + r − 2j tells whether the truss is statically determinate (0), indeterminate (> 0) or a mechanism (< 0); a singular K during the solve is reported as a mechanism, as Karamba does. For a determinate truss the member forces do not depend on section size, so they can be checked by hand with the method of joints.
