    Every system spans the same clear space. Structure zone is the height given to structure above the clear height. Space efficiency is the clear section L×H divided by the whole enclosed section — what the roof shape costs in volume to heat, light and clad. Footprint is the ground width the system needs, including arch feet, membrane edges and cable anchors.

    Notes — sources, rules of thumb and what this is not

    Classification and span ranges follow Heino Engel, Structure Systems (Tragsysteme), with his codes (e.g. 2.1.3 two-chord truss). The span table of each family is on pp. 62 (form-active), 138 (vector-active), 176 (section-active) and 216 (surface-active).

    Depths, rises and sags are rules of thumb — Engel's synopsis pages give spans but not depth ratios. The values below come from common preliminary-design ratios, cross-checked with Thomas Leslie's ARCH 445 lectures (one-way beams and trusses ~L/14–18, space frames L/20–25, long vaults and folded plates L/10–18). They give a first size for comparing schemes, not a design.

    Thrust is the horizontal force at the supports of arches, cables and domes for the roof load q, from H = qL²/8f (parabolic arch or cable) and the membrane ring force for a spherical dome. It has to go somewhere — a tie, buttresses, anchors or a ring — which is the architectural consequence the table points to.

    Not modelled: wind, snow drift, asymmetric load, buckling of arches and shells, dynamic behaviour of cables and membranes, the height-active family (multi-storey) and hybrids. The next step is to send a chosen scheme to a solver (see Truss Lab, study 11).
