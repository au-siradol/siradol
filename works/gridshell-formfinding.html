Net & hanging model

How the net is made A square grid is laid over the plan and trimmed at the edge. Every edge is held at its height, as Otto's chain model was held on posts (Fig 13–14, Happold & Liddell 1975). The laths are pin-jointed and a little longer than the plan grid, like the chain links, so the net hangs by changing shape, not by stretching; slack sets how deep it hangs. The links to the edge pull with one set force, as Otto's springs did, so the lath lines share the load. Each model member stands for several real 50 mm laths at 0.5 m, as the 1975 computer model used one member for many laths.

How the forces are found The shape is found for the permanent load: laths (5 kg/m² per layer: hemlock, 50 × 50 mm at 0.5 m both ways), bolts, blocks, nailing strips and ties (9 kg/m², set so the Mannheim double layer gives Liddell's 20 kg/m²) and the covering. It is uniform, so the shape does not depend on how heavy it is, only the forces do. Dynamic relaxation (karamba-lite.js) returns the equilibrium forces of the found shape: tension while hanging, the same values in compression once inverted; the reactions must add up to the weight. Karamba's Analyze Large Deformation returns no forces.

What the geometry tells A 50 mm lath breaks at a radius of about 10–12 m (Liddell 2015); where the shell curves tighter the laths were split into two 25 mm layers. The square grid curves by turning into rhombi: a large change of angle is where the covering and the ties have to follow the shear. A low rise over span makes a shell that buckles sooner.

How the loads are combined No imposed load is added. Stresses are checked for 1.35 G, and with snow also for 1.35 G + 1.5 S over the whole plan and over half of it (a shell suffers most from load on one side). This study uses 2.5 as a comparison target for verified equilibrium under unfactored loads, motivated by the historical study; it is not a complete modern design safety check. Wind is not included (Mannheim was tested in a wind tunnel).

What Analyze does First-order analysis (Karamba's Analyze) for every load combination: 1.35 G, and with snow 1.35 G + 1.5 S over the whole plan and over half of it. It reports the worst lath force and moment, the utilisation of the laths and edge members, and the deflection under G + S against span / 250. Buckling of the shell is not checked here, and in a gridshell buckling often governs: Mannheim was designed against a collapse load (Happold & Liddell 1975). How the suggestion is made A quick estimate of the trend, not a design. The shell is stiffened in the order it was at Mannheim and each step is analysed once, with its edge members sized from that one analysis: (1) one layer each way, as Essen; (2) two layers, their bolts giving the composite action measured in 1974 (joint shear stiffness at 40 % of the design value, γ 0.5 → 0.29); (3) shear blocks where the lath forces are highest (γ = 1 there); (4) diagonal cable ties, 2 × 6 mm through every sixth node; (5) a third layer (the 2018 renovation plan). A step passes when every lath and edge member is within its strength and the deflection under G + S within span / 250; the first that passes is suggested. The laths stay 50 × 50 mm, a size set by how far they bend. γ = 0.5 before the 40 % reduction is an assumption. Not checked: buckling of the shell.

How the members are sized Each group takes the smallest section in its list that passes every load combination: columns CHS 139.7–508 (combined stress and flexural buckling as vertical cantilevers, EC3 curve b), edge beams glulam 2 × 60 × 400–1200 and, when no glulam passes, steel CHS (as Stahl proposed in 2018), arches glulam, cables spiral strand. The analysis is repeated until the sizes settle, because a stiffer member draws more force. Indicative stress checks (glulam 14.8 MPa, S355, strand 0.45 fu), not a full Eurocode design.
Benchmarks (1975) Collapse load incl. own weight (Happold & Liddell 1975, Table 3): Multihalle single layer 3.8 · double 100 · double + ties 160 kg/m²; restaurant 5.8 · 150 · 240 kg/m². Computer run 105 kg/m². Load test: 40 kg/m² on 500 m² off centre, 79 mm measured against 70 mm (Liddell 2015) or 80 mm (Bauwelt 2017) computed. Maximum lath force about 750 kgf.
