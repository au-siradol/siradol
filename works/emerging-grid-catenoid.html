Structure & references

Structural principle. The built project combines a continuous reinforced-concrete shell with cores, facade walls and plug walls. Steel beams support inserted floors and seating; connections transfer their loads to the walls. Curved or inclined walls require this complete load path, not just a smooth shape. Evergreen: structural system.

Flexible grid. The layout is project-inspired: schematic proportions, not a traced construction plan. The 30% flange and 54° slope are study defaults, not measured project dimensions, and small cells limit the inset to preserve valid edges. The shell is drawn at a nominal 230 mm, locally limited at tight curves; it is not a design thickness.

Generative study. The four stages interpret the book's Emerging Grid sequence. Editable power cells, shared rim levels, catenary-inspired profiles and subdivision are approximations; they do not reproduce C6_B or guarantee a mathematical minimal surface.

Three different levels. Control grids define the shell; floor levels insert slabs; top/bottom cuts crop the study. The 120 × 60 m envelope, 15 m grid spacing, −15 m base cut and default floors are schematic. Reference drawings from different design stages are not combined into an as-built preset.

Design development. Void-forming proposals and later solid-concrete construction are distinct. A 2014 project account lists 400–800 mm solid walls and discusses local thickening to 1,000 mm. This study uses a nominal 230 mm display shell, with locally limited offsets at tight curves; it is not an as-built thickness map. D13@200 in the supplied detail labels formwork-fixing bars, not a universal truss spacing.

Built structure. The curved shell works with cores, plug walls, concrete facades and inserted structural systems. This interactive illustrates geometry and floor openings only; it does not calculate forces, reinforcement or structural capacity.
