1. Net Generation

A square grid is generated over the boundary and trimmed at every edge crossing, exactly as Atelier Warmbronn's chain-net grid was fixed only at its boundary connections. Interior nodes remain free — the lattice is a one-degree-of-freedom mechanism until curved (Fig. 8, Happold & Liddell 1975).
Note: this is a pedagogical approximation, not an engineering calculation. The real Mannheim analysis used non-linear Newton–Raphson buckling runs (Happold & Liddell 1975) and Linkwitz's force-density method for the exact geometry — both far beyond what a browser demo can reproduce.

2. Gravity Relaxation

By default the boundary is a fixed ring (matches a flat support system). With Free-Hanging Boundary on, only every Nth boundary node stays a hard support — the rest sag under gravity too, closer to how Otto adjusted boundary heights point-by-point against a support system to find the optimum shape (Fig 13–14, Happold & Liddell 1975) instead of holding the whole edge in one flat plane.
